BRAND DNA DOCUMENT
==================

## BRAND OVERVIEW

**NAME:** Polène (accent on the e)
**OFFICIAL TAGLINE / SELF-DESCRIPTION:** "Fine Leathercraft — Designed in Paris, Made in Spain"
**SECONDARY POSITIONING LINE:** "The House of Polène"

**FOUNDING STORY:**
Founded in 2016 in Paris by three siblings: Antoine, Mathieu, and Elsa Mothay. Their
great-grandfather Léon Legallais founded the iconic French brand Saint James (the striped
marinière favored by Picasso, Warhol, and Audrey Hepburn), giving the family a pedigree
in accessible, quality-forward French goods — though none of the three founders had
previously worked in fashion. After eight months of development and 25 design prototypes,
they launched Numéro Un, which became their signature best-seller. Production is based
entirely in Ubrique, Spain — Europe's leathercraft capital — with over 1,300 artisans.
Leather is sourced exclusively from Spanish and Italian certified tanneries. In September
2024, L Catterton (LVMH-backed private equity) acquired a minority stake.

**DESIGN AGENCY:** Violaine & Jérémy (Paris-based design studio)
— Commissioned for full brand identity redesign in 2023/2024, timed to the launch
  of Polène's first jewelry and accessories range.
— Deliverables: new custom wordmark logo (all-caps), monogram, official pattern,
  secondary typeface selection, and new color palette system.

**BOUTIQUE ARCHITECTURE:** Valeriane Lazard (French architect, Design Academy Eindhoven)
— Designed the Paris flagship (Rue de Richelieu) and New York SoHo boutique.
— Interior palette: cool beige plaster walls, travertine stone, deeply oiled oak parquet,
  walnut accents — warm, voluptuous minimalism.

**VOICE ADJECTIVES [5]:**
1. **SCULPTED** — language is tactile, material-forward; the leather "is sculpted to create organic shapes, folds, draping or weaving"
2. **RESTRAINED** — communications avoid superlatives; the brand "speaks in a low voice and lets the material speak for itself"
3. **PRECISE** — product copy is exacting about craft: hand-stitching, certified tanneries, named artisan regions; no vague claims
4. **TIMELESS** — copy consistently avoids trend language; frames products as "refined and timeless," never seasonal-hype driven
5. **INTIMATE** — brand storytelling centers the human hand: "it is the artisan behind each creation who speaks"; the brand treats buyer and maker as equals in a quiet dialogue

**BRAND POSITIONING:**
Accessible quiet luxury. Price range €300–€650. Polène occupies the precise gap between
fast-fashion leather goods and entry-level traditional luxury houses (Louis Vuitton, Celine).
The brand does not shout its logo; it competes on silhouette, material quality, and a
distinctly Parisian restraint. Internally, the brand describes its design language as
"épuré" — refined/pure in French.

**COMPETITIVE DIFFERENTIATION:**

| Competitor | Similarity | Polène Differentiator |
|---|---|---|
| DeMellier (London) | ~$350–$650 price range | DeMellier leans British polish and cause-driven messaging. Polène wins on sculptural silhouette originality and French heritage. |
| A.P.C. (Paris) | Similar minimalism | A.P.C. is fashion-led with seasonal drops. Polène is leathercraft-led with numbered permanent collections. |
| The Row (New York) | Sculptural quiet-luxury aesthetic | The Row operates at 10x the price point. Polène democratizes the same visual grammar. |

**UNFAIR ADVANTAGE:** TikTok-native organic growth (#polene: 210M+ views; 143% YoY
increase 2023→2024) without compromising brand tone. Featured organically in Emily in
Paris (Netflix). Worn by Kate Middleton and Meghan Markle without paid celebrity endorsement.

## VISUAL SYSTEM

**PRIMARY FONT — WORDMARK / DISPLAY:**
Custom all-caps lettering (commissioned by Violaine & Jérémy, 2024)
Character: sharp, confident, graceful. Clean geometric-yet-organic capital letter forms.
No serifs on the wordmark itself. Appears in single-weight, widely tracked spacing.
Not commercially available — proprietary.

**SECONDARY FONT — EDITORIAL HEADLINES / BODY SERIF:**
Canela by Commercial Type
Classification: Contemporary serif, slightly condensed, high contrast strokes.
Weight used: Light to Regular for editorial display, not bold.
Usage: Feature headings on editorial pages, campaign text overlays, long-form brand storytelling sections.

**TERTIARY FONT — SUBHEADINGS / LABELS / UI:**
Sackers Gothic by Monotype
Classification: All-caps geometric sans-serif, delicate hairline-weight.
Character: Quiet, aristocratic, evenly spaced. The quintessential "luxury label" typeface.
Usage: Category labels, navigation items, price callouts, product sub-labels.
Letter-spacing: Significantly wide-tracked (typical +200 to +400 tracking units).

**QUATERNARY FONT — BODY / PRODUCT DESCRIPTIONS:**
Cormorant Garamond (Google Fonts / open source, designed by Christian Thalmann)
Classification: High-contrast old-style serif, inspired by Claude Garamond.
Weight used: Light or Regular. Never bold in body copy.
Usage: Product description paragraphs, about page body text, editorial long-form.
Note: Pairs directly with Sackers Gothic in the luxury fashion typographic tradition.

**TYPOGRAPHY HIERARCHY:**
| Level | Font | Treatment |
|---|---|---|
| H1 / Hero | Custom Polène wordmark OR Canela Light | Large, wide-tracked |
| H2 / Section head | Canela Light or Regular | Medium size, generous line height |
| H3 / Product name | Sackers Gothic | All-caps, widely tracked |
| Body | Cormorant Garamond Light | Comfortable reading size, generous leading |
| Labels/Nav | Sackers Gothic | All-caps, wide-tracked, hairline weight |

**DISTINCTIVE TYPOGRAPHIC TREATMENTS:**
— The "è" accent in Polène is always preserved; the accent is a brand asset
— All navigation uses Sackers Gothic in all-caps
— Product names follow the "Numéro [Number]" naming convention (Numéro Un, Numéro Neuf, Numéro Dix, Cyme, Hors-Champ)
— Accent marks and French nomenclature are retained even on English-language site

**PRIMARY COLOR — BACKGROUND / CANVAS:**
Near-white / warm ivory cream
`#FAF8F5` (warm ivory) or `#F7F4F0` (cream white)
Application: Website background, packaging ground color, photography background.
Note: Not pure white (#FFFFFF) — always carries a warm, slightly yellowed undertone that reads as "natural" and "artisanal."

**SECONDARY COLOR — TEXT / PRIMARY UI:**
Deep charcoal / near-black (not pure black)
`#1A1A1A` or `#2A2725` (warm dark brown-black)
Application: All body text, navigation, product names, price points.
Note: Warm-toned black, not cool blue-black. Complements the cream background.

**ACCENT COLOR — BRAND GREY:**
Medium warm grey / boulder grey
`#767676` (documented in Brandfetch brand assets)
Alternative tonal reference: `#8C8480` (warm greige)
Application: Secondary text, metadata, inactive states, borders, dividers.

**BACKGROUND COLORS:**
- Primary canvas: Warm ivory `#FAF8F5`
- Secondary/editorial: Soft taupe or warm greige `#EDE8DF`
- Dark mode / campaign accent: Deep charcoal `#1A1A1A` (used sparingly in editorials)

**CTA COLOR AND STYLE:**
- Primary CTA: Black or deep charcoal fill (`#1A1A1A`) with white text
- Secondary CTA: Ghost/outline style — thin border in charcoal, transparent fill, dark text
- Button style: Minimal, no border-radius (sharp or very subtly rounded corners)
- Button typography: Sackers Gothic, all-caps, wide-tracked
- CTA language: Restrained, non-pushy — "Discover," "Explore," "View the Collection"
- Never: "Buy Now," "Shop Now," "Add to Cart" urgency language at hero level

## PHOTOGRAPHY DIRECTION

**LIGHTING:**
Soft, diffused natural or studio-simulated daylight. Key light placed to the side or
slightly above to create gentle, directional shadow that gives three-dimensionality to the
leather's texture and surface. Avoids harsh contrast or blown-out highlights. Boutique design
inspiration: "natural shadows and intimate atmosphere" — soft indoor lighting with subtle
surface reflections. The lighting reads as "painterly."
Never: ring light flatness, harsh strobe shadows, or high-contrast fashion lighting.

**COLOR GRADING:**
Warm and muted. Slight warm tone lift in midtones (+5 to +10 warmth). Shadows are warm
(brown-black, not cool blue-black). Highlights are held — never pure white blowout.
Overall feel: "bleached linen in afternoon light." Slight desaturation of competing colors so
that the leather's natural warmth is always the dominant warm tone in the frame.
Skin tones (in lifestyle shots): warm, luminous, never orange. Think muted film look, not
digital oversaturation.

**COMPOSITION:**
Generous negative space. Products placed off-center or centered with significant breathing
room. Minimal props — the product is always the clear subject.
Layout: Often portrait-format for product shots (2:3 ratio), square for social (1:1).
Close-up details are prominent — texture macro shots of leather grain, hand-stitching,
hardware, gusset folds. These detail shots are used heavily in both editorial and ads.
Model shots: Often partial — hands holding bag, bag resting on arm, bag against body without
full face in shot. When face is included: neutral, composed expression, not smiling. Gaze
directed away from camera or downcast — introspective, not commercial.
Negative space: frequently 40–60% of total frame. The brand never crowd-fills.

**SUBJECT MATTER:**
- Primary: The bag alone — on a clean surface, against neutral background, or in a carefully art-directed natural setting (stone sill, linen cloth, raw travertine surface).
- Secondary: Bag in lifestyle context — a composed, minimal Parisian apartment, a stone staircase, a marble café table. Always understated. Never aspirational-excess imagery (no yachts, champagne, obvious wealth signaling).
- Tertiary: Craft detail — artisan hands working leather in Ubrique workshop, close-up of tools, leather being folded, stitching in progress.

**PROPS AND SURFACES:**
Approved: Raw travertine, unpolished marble, warm oak wood, linen/cotton fabric,
terracotta tile, white plaster wall, aged stone, organic ceramic vessels, pressed cotton.
Colors of surfaces: Always within the brand's neutral universe — ivory, stone, warm grey,
oiled oak, raw sienna earth tones.
Rejected: Velvet luxury backdrops, metallic surfaces, busy patterns, bright saturated
backgrounds, overtly styled "maximalist" tablescapes.

**MOOD:**
Calm. Still. Considered. "A pause." The photography should feel like a quiet Saturday morning
in a Parisian apartment — unhurried, warm, tasteful. Not cold minimalism (which reads
clinical) but warm minimalism (which reads artisanal). Sophisticated without effort. The bags
look like they belong in this world — they are not placed; they exist.

## PRODUCT DETAILS

**PHYSICAL DESCRIPTION OF BAGS:**
Form: Sculptural, organic silhouettes. Most signature shapes feature curved edges, soft
pleating, gentle folds, and fluid draping — all achieved through hand-stitching. The bag
forms read as "sculpted" rather than "constructed." Architectural but tactile.
- The Numéro Un has a half-moon/quarter-circle silhouette with signature vertical fold.
- The Cyme has a soft, rounded bottom with top handle and structured flap.

Hardware: Minimal gold-tone or polished silver hardware. Clasps, D-rings, and buckles are
understated — never logo-embossed or decorative for their own sake.
Size range: Nano (micro/micro-mini), Mini, and standard — all named sizes, not S/M/L.
Material: Full-grain Italian calfskin leather. Two primary textures: smooth grain and textured
grain (pebbled). Also a "Textured" finish and limited edition materials.

**LABEL / LOGO PLACEMENT:**
- Exterior: NO visible exterior logo on bag body. The shape IS the brand mark.
- Interior: "POLÈNE PARIS" debossed or blind-stamped on interior leather panel.
- Post-2023 rebrand packaging: Boxes feature invisible emboss of "POLÈNE" only — no color ink logo, just tactile impression on the white box surface.
- Dust bag: "POLÈNE" or "POLÈNE PARIS" in tone-on-tone or contrasting minimal print.

**DISTINCTIVE FEATURES:**
— Numbered naming convention: Collections called Numéro Un through Numéro Dix+, plus newer named pieces (Cyme, Hors-Champ). The number is a product identifier, not a product line number — each Numéro is a distinct silhouette.
— Signature fold: The central visual motif across the collection. Leather is gathered, pleated, or draped into a fold that creates the defining shape of each piece.
— No exterior logo: Radical for the luxury segment. The brand competes on silhouette recognition alone — no monogram, no logo stamp.
— Color-first editions: Limited colorway drops (e.g., "Edition Cognac," "Spring-Summer Palette") rather than full seasonal product lines. Color is the edition story.

**PACKAGING SYSTEM:**

PRIMARY BOX:
- Form: Rigid box, square/rectangular format depending on bag size
- Color: Clean white with a warm ivory undertone — not clinical bright white
- Finish: Matte exterior; post-2023: invisible debossed "POLÈNE" wordmark only
- Closure: Rigid lid-and-base (not magnetic fold; no ribbon tie visible on exterior)
- Inside: White acid-free tissue paper wrapping the bag; secondary bubble wrap layer
- Feel: "The most well-packaged item I've ever received in the mail" (reviewer consensus)

DUST BAG:
- Material: High-quality woven cotton or felt-exterior fabric; notably heavier/denser than competitor dust bags (described by reviewers as "weighty")
- Color: Warm off-white / ivory or light taupe/stone
- Logo: "POLÈNE" or "POLÈNE PARIS" in simple sans-serif print, tone-on-tone or minimal contrast
- Closure: Drawstring

JEWELRY PACKAGING (distinctive innovation):
- Form: Pebble-shaped box inspired by organic, nature-found forms
- Material: 100% recycled paper pulp — molded, single-material construction (no glue)
- Available in three sizes for different jewelry types
- Interior: 100% cotton pouch for the jewelry piece itself
- Brand values expressed: Sustainability through material choice; sculptural design philosophy extended even to packaging

ADDITIONAL PACKAGING ELEMENTS:
— Brand tag / authenticity certificate enclosed
— No excessive branded tissue layers or decorative filler

## AD CREATIVE STYLE

**TYPICAL FORMATS:**
— Single static product image (bag alone on clean surface) — most common format
— Single static lifestyle image (bag with model or in aspirational environment)
— Short-form video (Instagram Reels, TikTok): product texture close-ups, slow-motion leather handling, bag reveal or outfit integration — no voiceover, music-forward
— Carousel: multiple colorway presentation of single silhouette, or detail + lifestyle
— Story format: vertical, minimal — often single image with minimal text

**TEXT OVERLAY STYLE:**
— Extremely minimal. Often ZERO text overlay on image.
— When text appears: small, elegant, positioned at bottom or corner
— Typography of overlays: Sackers Gothic all-caps, white or deep charcoal on image
— Copy when used: product name + "POLÈNE" wordmark only, or single short line
— No promotional callouts (no "20% OFF" banners, no discount overlays)
— Paid ad identification does not compromise the editorial feel of the creative

**PHOTO VS. ILLUSTRATION:**
100% photography. No illustration, no flat-lay graphics, no CGI renders.
The tactile, material reality of the leather is the primary sell — illustration would undermine this.

**UGC USAGE:**
Significant driver of organic growth. The brand leverages creator/influencer content heavily on TikTok and Instagram, where the #polene hashtag has 210M+ views.
UGC style: unboxing videos (slow reveal of white box, tissue paper, dust bag), outfit integration ("what fits in my Polène"), before/after wear-in reviews.
Polène runs an affiliate program (3% commission) to formalize the UGC ecosystem.
Key difference: Official content = product as art. UGC content = product as life.

**OFFER PRESENTATION:**
The brand does not run traditional promotional advertising. No "sale" or discount language appears in primary ad creative. Offers (when they exist) are presented as "limited edition colorway" or "new arrival" rather than price-driven calls to action.
Scarcity is implied through the numbered naming system and limited colorways — not manufactured urgency. The brand positions value through craft narrative, not price comparison.
